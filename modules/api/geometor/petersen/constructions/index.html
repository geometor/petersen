geometor.petersen.constructions¶

Exact symbolic constructions from Julius Petersen’s Methods and Theories (1866 / 1879).

Every construction in this module strictly obeys the GEOMETOR Symbolic Integrity Iron Law:

  1. Zero Floating-Point Contamination: Coordinates and parameters live strictly in \(\mathbb{Q}\) or closed-form quadratic radical extensions (\(\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{3})\), etc.). float and sympy.Float are never injected into a Model.

  2. Targeted Compass-and-Straightedge DAGs: Models run with auto_intersect=False, using explicit intersect() calls and subconstruction() contexts for auxiliary Euclidean steps.

  3. Constructive Propositions & Rational Trigonometry: Each method is structured as a @proposition workflow (Given -> Construct -> Demonstrate) verified via model.assert_*, quadrance, spread, and cross_ratio.

Inscribe a square DEFG resting on base AB of ABC via homothety from A.

Following Petersen’s Method of Similitude (Case b, p. 28–29, Problem 155), relaxing the condition that the upper-right vertex lie on BC makes all candidate squares resting on AB with upper-left vertex on AC homothetic from centre of similitude A. Constructing the prototype square D0 E0 F0 C of side equal to altitude h_c and intersecting ray A F0 with BC yields F directly.

Default (15, 13, 14) Heronian triangle A=(0,0), B=(14,0), C=(5,12) yields exact rational inscribed square side 84/13 with vertices D=(35/13, 0), E=(119/13, 0), F=(119/13, 84/13), G=(35/13, 84/13).

Place an equilateral triangle PQR with vertices on three parallel lines L1, L2, L3.

Following Petersen’s Theory of Revolution (Ch. III, § 1–4 & Problem 349, pp. 65–67), choose P on L1 as the pivot point with angle of revolution v = 60° and ratio of revolution f = 1. Revolving L2 by +60° around P via two Euclidean I.1 equilateral subconstructions produces the rotated line L2', whose intersection with L3 determines R; revolving back (I.1 on PR) yields Q on L2 in \(\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{3})\).

Construct trapezoid ABCD (AB || DC, AB=a, CD=b, BC=c, DA=d) via translation.

Following Petersen’s Parallel Translation (Ch. II.A, Problem 259, p. 49), translating non-parallel leg BC parallel to base CD so B -> E and C -> D gathers the four dispersed lengths into the constructible triangle AED with known sides AE = a - b, AD = d, and ED = c.

With default Heronian lengths (a=19, b=5, c=13, d=15), auxiliary triangle AED has sides (14, 15, 13) and every vertex lies on the exact integer lattice: A=(0,0), E=(14,0), B=(19,0), D=(9,12), C=(14,12).

Find points P with PA : PB = m : n at given distance h from line AB.

Combines Petersen’s Locus f (the Circle of Apollonius on harmonic diameter CC1 where CA:CB = C1A:C1B = m:n and (A, B; C, C1) = -1, pp. 11–12) with Locus b (parallel line at distance h from AB, p. 8) under the Method of Two Loci (p. 5).

Default A=(0,0), B=(15,0), m:n=2:1, h=8 yields internal/external harmonic points C=(10,0), C1=(30,0), Apollonius circle centered at M=(20,0) of radius 10, and exact integer intersections P1=(14,8) and P2=(26,8).

Construct a circle touching three circles K1, K2, K3 concurrent at O via inversion.

Following Petersen’s Inverse Figures (pp. 33–36, Problem 199):

  1. Invert K1, K2, K3 (all passing through O) in circle Omega(O, R0). Because each circle passes through the centre of inversion O, their inverse curves are three straight lines L1, L2, L3 forming triangle V1 V2 V3.

  2. Construct the incircle omega' of V1 V2 V3 (Euclid IV.4) touching L1, L2, L3 at T1', T2', T3'.

  3. Invert T1', T2', T3' back across Omega to obtain the three points of contact T1, T2, T3 and circumscribe the tangent circle omega (center J).

With default integer lattice parameters O=(0,0), M1=(-12,0), M2=(0,-9), M3=(12,9), R0=12 (radii 12, 9, 15), L1, L2, L3 form an (18, 24, 30) right triangle with incenter J'=(0,-2), inradius 6, and inverted tangent circle omega centered at J=(0,9) of exact integer radius 27!

Construct right triangles on hypotenuse BC = a with given altitude h_a (Loci e & b).

Illustrates Petersen’s canonical 4-part anatomy of a problem of construction (Analysis, Mode of Proceeding, Proof, Discussion, pp. 4–5) via Problem 42 (p. 15): Divide a given segment BC into two parts BD, DC having a given segment h as mean proportional. Intersecting the Thales circle on diameter BC (Locus e, 90°) with the parallel line at distance h (Locus b) yields:

  • 2 solutions A1, A2 when 0 < h < a/2 (default a=10, h=4 -> A1=(2,4), A2=(8,4)),

  • 1 tangent solution when h = a/2, and

  • 0 real solutions when h > a/2.

geometor.petersen.constructions.equal_chords_line_locus(model: geometor.model.Model | None = None, O: tuple[Any, Any] | sympy.geometry.Point2D = (0, 0), outer_radius: int | sympy.Expr = 17, chord_length: int | sympy.Expr = 16, P: tuple[Any, Any] | sympy.geometry.Point2D = (25, 0)) → tuple[geometor.model.Model, dict[str, Any]]¶

Through external point P draw lines intercepting a chord of length L in circle (O, R).

Following Petersen’s Loci of Lines (Locus l, p. 40 & Problem 220, p. 42), all straight lines cutting chords of length L from circle (O, R) lie at fixed distance \(r = \sqrt{R^2 - (L/2)^2}\) from O and therefore envelop the concentric circle (O, r). Drawing tangents from P to (O, r) (Problem 16 / Euclid III.17) solves the problem immediately.

Default triple-Pythagorean parameters R=17, L=16, P=(25,0) give concentric envelope radius r=15 (8^2 + 15^2 = 17^2), exact integer contact points T1=(9,12) and T2=(9,-12) (15^2 + 20^2 = 25^2), and exact rational chord endpoints on (O, 17).

Find X on a given line so AX and BX make equal angles with the line (Prob. 338).

Following Petersen’s Revolution Around an Axis (p. 60–61, Problem 338), revolving A across the mirror axis into A1 straightens the reflected path into the single segment A1 X B. With default A=(2,6), B=(14,10) and axis y=0, A1=(2,-6), X=(13/2, 0), and Q(A1, B) = 12^2 + 16^2 = 400 = 20^2!
