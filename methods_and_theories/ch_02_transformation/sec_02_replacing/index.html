B. Replacing (pp. 60–64, Problems 309–335)¶

[pp. 56–57] Replacing rearranges a sub-figure into a flipped or proportionally scaled congruent/similar position to achieve one of three ends:

  1. To gather the given parts (Problem 311, p. 56): Inscribing a quadrilateral \(ABCD\) in a given circle given opposite sides \(AB, CD\) and the ratio \(BC : AD\) — flip \(\triangle ABC\) by interchanging \(A\) and \(C\) on the circle so the two known sides become adjacent!

  2. To introduce given sums or differences of angles/sides (Problem 313, p. 57): Construct \(\triangle ABC\) from \(a, b\), and \(A - B\) by replacing \(\triangle ABC\) with \(A\) and \(B\) interchanged.

  3. To make equal or proportional unknown lines cover each other (Problem 316, p. 57): Construct an inscribable (cyclic) quadrilateral \(ABCD\) whose four sides \(a, b, c, d\) are given — scale \(\triangle ABC\) by \(d : a\) and flip it into position \(ADC_1\); because \(\angle B + \angle D = 180^\circ\), \(DC\) and \(DC_1\) become collinear, eliminating the unknown circumcircle and yielding \(\triangle CAC_1\) directly via Locus f!
